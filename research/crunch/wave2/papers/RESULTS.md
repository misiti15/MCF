## Sources read (pages actually opened)

### 1. Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka, "Intraday Patterns in the Cross-section of Stock Returns", JF 2010 — [arXiv 1005.3535](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.3535) (W6 #6, now read in full)
At lags that are whole days (13, 26, ... half-hours), a stock's half-hour return continues for at least 40 trading days. The top-minus-bottom decile spread is 3.01 bps in the same half-hour on the next day (1993-2005 TAQ data). That is about twice the 1.7 bps quoted half-spread of S&P 500 names, so it is mainly an execution-timing effect, not a standalone trade. The finding that matters most for MCF concerns **short-term reversal**. Using bid-to-bid, ask-to-ask and mid-to-mid returns, the only significant reversal is at lag 1 (30-60 minutes). All the reversal seen in trade prices beyond one hour is bid-ask bounce. **Implication:** an intraday mean-reversion trigger on trade-price closes will partly be bounce. Only reversal that completes within about an hour reflects real liquidity resiliency. MCF's LR8 costs already charge the spread. **Testable?** Weakly. Fade setups whose median hold is longer than 60 minutes could be checked for edge beyond one hour, but this overlaps C3-EXIT100 and the exit studies, so no new backlog line. W6-HKS (same-slot continuation) stays as proposed. guild-1117-lasthalfhour already covers the late-day version.

### 2. Vidovich & Zerjal, "The End-of-Day Reversal After 2019: A Pre-Registered Test on S&P 500 Stocks Excluding Heavily Traded Names", Nov 2026 — [arXiv 2611.09286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09286)
The paper replicates the Baltussen-Da-Soebhag end-of-day reversal: intraday losers (prior close to 15:00) beat intraday winners from 15:30 to 16:00. **Year by year (primary sample, bps/day, t):** 2016-19 positive every year (2.7-4.0). **2020 -0.82, 2021 -0.68, 2022 1.38 (none significant). 2023 6.62 (t 6.05), the strongest year in the sample, which supplies 56% of the 2023-26 estimate. 2024 0.85 (0.58), 2025 2.10 (1.05), 2026 2.58 (0.78).** Without 2023, the 2024-26 mean is 1.80 bps (t 1.37), and 2023 differs from 2024-26 at gap t 2.82. On the full index with dollar-volume weights, the 2023-26 effect is only 0.74 bps (t 0.55). The weakness sits in the ~80 largest names (3.88 → 0.33 bps). The leveraged-ETF launch test points the predicted way (-3.5 bps, t -1.3) but could not be judged (12 treated stocks against 20 required). The authors make no claim net of costs. **Implication for MCF's regime problem:** in at least one well-documented intraday reversal, **2023 was an outlier year**, and 2024-26 looks like the weak 2020-22 regime. Mean-reversion setups that looked strong on 2023-24 bars (cache_fresh) and then faded in 2025-26 fit this pattern. That does not have to mean the setups broke: 2023 may have flattered them. **Testable:** yes, as a reporting and selection rule. Lab and fresh-data reviews of fade families would show R per calendar year and a "one-big-year" share, the same idea as the one-big-day check (backlog: papers-1119-one-big-year). The reversal itself (about 3 bps before costs, in mid/large caps) is far below MCF's per-trade cost, so it is not proposed as a setup.

### 3. Zhao, "Preying on Leveraged ETFs", Aug 2026 — [arXiv 2608.03703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03703)
An L-times daily-reset fund must trade L(L-1) × the day's return at the close. **L(L-1) is positive for inverse funds too, and for a -3x fund it is 12, twice the 6 of a +3x fund.** So the closing flow always goes in the direction of the underlying's move. In Korea's 2026 single-stock LETFs, where the mandated rebalance averaged 22.4% of SK Hynix's daily value, speculators front-ran the flow, 75% of the first-day news response reversed by the next close, and volatility rose from 100% to 136.7%. The paper finds **no such reversal in U.S. index complexes or U.S. stocks with LETFs**, because their loop gain is small. **Implication:** for U.S. inverse index ETFs such as SQQQ and SPXS, the main thing is not microstructure. It is that the product carries the underlying's beta times L. A short on SQQQ is a +3x long on Nasdaq, plus an end-of-day flow that adds to the market's direction. This supports rootcause_1119's reading of L3's 11-19 loss and the wave2/inverse_guard remedy (G1 live +78.8R, 50 better / 24 worse; lab with the ETB proxy +79.6R). **Testable:** already covered by nightly-1102-skip-inverse-etf-shorts, nightly-1102-l3-inverse-etf-skip, bdi-1102-underlying-exposure-cap and inverse_guard. No new line.

### 4. Murray & Sammon, "The Costs and Benefits of Leveraged ETFs", Oct 2026 — [arXiv 2611.01392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01392)
Volatility drag grows with L(L-1)σ² and applies to inverse funds as well. Over their sample, U.S. inverse LETFs took $93.4B of net flows and lost investors $80.3B (112 funds), while long LETFs gained $114.8B. Single-stock LETFs launch on high-volatility, high-past-return names, where the drag is about 7.6× that of index LETFs (3.71% vs 1.35% daily volatility in 2025). **Implication:** inverse ETFs fall steadily over many days (drag plus rising markets). A gap/slope fade scanner like L3 will therefore keep "qualifying" them: on 11-19, all 42 symbols L3 qualified in Primary were inverse products. That multi-day decay does nothing for a trade held for minutes or hours. **Testable:** no new line; it backs the inverse guard.

### 5. QuantPedia, "Shorting Leveraged ETFs: The Role of Borrowing Costs and Rebalancing in Volatility-Decay Strategies", 23 Oct 2026 — [quantpedia.com](https://quantpedia.com/shorting-leveraged-etfs-the-role-of-borrowing-costs-and-rebalancing-in-volatility-decay-strategies/)
This tests a 50/50 short of TQQQ and SQQQ (2015-2026), which is market-neutral and harvests the decay. With no fees and rebalancing at the close: 3.92%/yr, Sharpe 0.77. **With actual borrow fees: 2.09%/yr, Sharpe 0.41.** With the rebalance 10 minutes before the close: **-1.3%/yr, Sharpe -0.57.** The returns sit in a few high-volatility periods. **Implication:** even the best case for shorting inverse ETFs is a small, fee-sensitive, multi-day, hedged trade that is very sensitive to timing. A one-sided intraday short gets none of it. This is consistent with the inverse guard. No new line.

### 6. Vidovich & Zerjal, "Where Backtests and Live Trading Part Ways: Six Defects from One Intraday System", Sep 2026 — [arXiv 2610.21846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21846)
The paper reports six defects found by checking that backtest and live agree. Three came from data: a default IEX feed with 0.1-2% of real opening-minute volume, dividend-adjusted history set against unadjusted fills, and an input the live system downloaded but the replay did not, which silently read zero for 4 months. Two came from code: a later sizing step that pushed size past the buying-power limit (20 of 61 fills, about 18% too large), and UTC strings read as New York time (13:30 UTC is 08:30 ET in winter, so 16% of winter stock-mornings were scored on a pre-market bar and 84% were dropped). One came from configuration: rules switched on in config but never reaching the order path. The authors' lesson is that every defect sat where two paths that should agree had drifted apart, and that summary statistics never showed it. **Implication for MCF:** the RSI artifact (S3) and the broker stop-rounding bug are this same kind of defect. **US daylight saving time ended on 2026-11-01**, inside every current live window. A quick grep of mcf/ found no hard-coded "13:30"/"14:30" open strings, and 38 modules use America/New_York or ZoneInfo. That is not a proof. **Testable:** housekeeping, not a setup idea. Add one winter date and one summer date to the bar-time tests, pin the data feed (SIP) and price adjustment (raw) in every fetch, and check `bdi-tod-sma50-warmup-parity` (live carries 40 prior bars, lab more) as the same live/lab drift. It is listed here for the housekeeping agent and gets no backlog line, because RESEARCH_RULES 15-17 send setup ideas to the backlog, not housekeeping.

### 7. Robot Wealth (K. Longmore), "Using Exponentially Weighted Moving Averages to navigate trade-offs in systematic trading", Nov 2023 — [robotwealth.com](https://robotwealth.com/using-exponentially-weighted-moving-averages-to-navigate-trade-offs-in-systematic-trading/)
A recursive smoother's early values depend on very little history and must be thrown away during a warm-up (the post uses 20 observations for the mean, and a 90-252-observation initialisation window for EW correlation). **How this maps to MCF's two RSI implementations (read here in code):** `mcf/features.py::rsi` is Wilder-style (`ewm(alpha=1/n, adjust=False)`). A prior-close-to-open gap fades out of it gradually, but its value depends on how many prior bars are carried (the warm-up issue in the post). `mcf/research/arena/indicators.py::_rsi` is a **simple n-bar rolling sum (Cutler's RSI)**. Here the overnight gap change leaves the window abruptly after exactly n bars. With n = 14 on 5-minute bars that is the 10:40 bar, which closes at 10:45: the 11-19 ST7-FD burst. **Testable:** yes. Remove the session-boundary cliff at its source. Score, per RSI-based setup family, (a) RSI on intraday changes only (the prior close → first bar change excluded) and (b) Wilder smoothing in place of the rolling mean, against the current indicator. rootcause_1119 already showed that skipping the 10:45 bar is worse (ST7 -35.6R, ST7Q -62.6R). The new line is papers-1119-rsi-session-boundary.

### 8. Miwa, "Short-Term Return Reversals and Intraday Transactions" (via QuantPedia summary, 29 May 2018) — [quantpedia.com](https://quantpedia.com/short-term-return-reversals-and-intraday-transactions/)
Only the QuantPedia page was opened; the SSRN original (3174484) is blocked. The short-term reversal comes from **intraday** returns. **Overnight returns do not reverse**, in US, Japan, UK and Eurozone stocks. The intraday reversal is stronger in illiquid names and when the VIX is high, and it is not stronger around news, which points to liquidity provision rather than overreaction. **Implication:** this matches W6's P1: fading a ≥ 2% gap loses (-0.157 / -0.187R in the two halves, and -0.376 / -0.596 in Primary), while trading with the gap is about flat to positive. It also matches W6-MFGAP (MF flowsell shorts that fade a gap-up: 127 trades at -0.558R). Many fade triggers measure the stretch from the **prior close** (RSI with the gap included, distance from prior close, ROD3-type moves), which mixes the overnight part that does not revert with the intraday part that does. **Testable:** yes, and it is a targeted family-level remedy. For fade/mean-reversion setups, measure the stretch from **today's open** (the intraday part only) and leave the gap out. The new line is papers-1119-fade-intraday-component. It is related to the RSI fix (item 7) but separate: item 7 removes a time cliff, and item 8 changes what the stretch measures.

### 9. QuantPedia, "Correlation Acceleration: Detecting Market Stress Through Rising ETF Correlations", 6 Nov 2026 — [quantpedia.com](https://quantpedia.com/correlation-acceleration-detecting-market-stress-through-rising-etf-correlations/)
The average pairwise correlation of the 9 sector ETFs over 20 days, compared against 60 days, is used as a one-day short-SPY hedge trigger. Only a wide threshold region (corr20 > 1.12-1.25 × corr60) helps: Sharpe 0.53-0.60 against 0.52 for SPY, and max drawdown -38% to -43% against -55%. Low thresholds give up most of the return. **Implication for stacking:** concentration risk is when positions that are normally different start to move as one. On 11-19, 13 of 14 losing lots were effectively long Nasdaq/S&P beta. The daily signal is too slow for an intraday book, and rootcause_1119 found stacking caps cost L3/RW3/RW5 13-21R each. **Testable:** not as a cap, which would conflict with the owner's stacking position and with the 11-19 finding. The beta-weighted net-exposure idea already exists (swing1103-L4-net-beta-cap, bdi-1102-underlying-exposure-cap). No new line.
