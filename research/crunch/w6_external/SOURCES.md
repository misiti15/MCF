# W6-external — outside sources: the 10 most applicable ideas

Educational only — not financial advice.

**How the sources were read.** Every source below was found with WebSearch on 2026-11-18. WebFetch could not open any page: every host tried (en.wikipedia.org, ar5iv.arxiv.org, alphaarchitect.com, cxoadvisory.com, docs.alpaca.markets, a CRAN mirror) failed to resolve, because these hosts are outside this session's allowed network domains. **No page was opened in full.** Each idea is therefore cited from the abstract or summary text that the search returned, and every figure below is labelled as coming from that text. Nothing here should be quoted as a verified reading of the full paper. To open the papers, the owner would need to allow those hosts in the environment's network settings.

| 1 | **Deflated Sharpe ratio**: Bailey & López de Prado, *JPM* 40(5), 2014, [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551) | The best Sharpe among N trials is inflated, and its expected value rises with N. The DSR corrects for the number of trials, non-normal returns and sample length. | Our leaderboard ranks 210 live setups by raw records, with no deflation. | X2 |
| 2 | **Probability of backtest overfitting (CSCV)**: Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, *J. Computational Finance*, [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253) | Split the sample into S blocks and use every half/half combination. PBO is the share of splits in which the in-sample winner ranks below the median out of sample. The paper says plain hold-out is unreliable for backtests. | Is "promote the best record" better than chance? | X1 |
| 3 | **Haircut Sharpe ratios**: Harvey & Liu, "Backtesting", [Duke P120](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF) (via [Man Institute summary](https://www.man.com/insights/backtesting)) | A t of 3.0 can lose significance once the number of tests is counted. The haircut is nonlinear: marginal Sharpes are cut heavily, and a flat 50% haircut is wrong. | Same as rule 19's try-count bar max(1.5, sqrt(2 ln N)). Apply it to live records, not only backtests. | X2 |
| 4 | **Breadth with correlated bets**: Grinold-Kahn fundamental law; Clarke, de Silva & Thorley "implied breadth" (via [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-int/insights/2018/04/fundamental-law-of-active-management-shows-way-to-higher-information-ratio) and [CFA Institute reading](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2025/analysis-active-portfolio-management)) | IR ≈ TC × IC × √breadth. Correlated bets add less breadth than their count. The law ignores costs. | Many weak edges help only if they are independent. How many independent bets does Primary hold? | X7 |
| 5 | **Market intraday momentum**: Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, *JFE* 129(2), 2018, 394-414 ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414)) | SPY's first half-hour return, overnight included, predicts its last half-hour return (R² about 0.02, per the summary). A [QuantConnect replication](https://www.quantconnect.com/learning/articles/investment-strategy-library/intraday-etf-momentum) found weaker returns in a later period. | Does the market's direction so far help predict MCF's late-day trades? Does it help predict early trades? | X5, Q1 |
| 6 | **Intraday periodicity**: Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka, *JF* 65(4), 2010 ([arXiv 1005.3535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.3535.pdf), [Kellogg page](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2010/intraday-patterns-in-the-cross-section-of-stock-returns)) | A stock's half-hour return reverses at short lags but continues at lags of whole days (the same half-hour on later days), for up to 40 days. Size and S&P membership do not explain it. | Feature idea: the stock's return in the same 30-minute slot on prior days, as a setup condition. | not run (needs 1-min packs; proposed W6-HKS) |
| 7 | **Overnight-to-intraday (gap) reversal has decayed** in liquid names: Branch & Ma retrospective (via [CXO guide](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/guides/intraday-overnight/)); [Turku 2011-2024 thesis](https://www.utupub.fi/bitstreams/88057d2a-1ee8-4c05-9592-c535a21b502c/download); [Concretum note](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/identifying-stocks-to-fade) | The reversal now survives only in small, illiquid, hard-to-exploit stocks and in high-uncertainty periods. For S&P 500 names the overnight-intraday relation is now positive. The thesis calls it structural rather than exploitable. | Our setups that fade the open gap vs. those that trade with it. | X3, P1, MF check |
| 8 | **Short-selling risk and squeezes**: Engelberg, Reed & Ringgenberg, *JF* 2018; Schultz, *JFQA* 2024; Henderson, Jostova & Philipov (via [Swedroe/ETF.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-risks-short-selling)) | Squeezes are more common in small, hard-to-borrow names. High lending-fee risk is concentrated in micro and small caps. Most "special" stocks do not underperform. | Do our shorts take squeeze-type tail losses after run-ups or gap-ups? | X4 |
| 9 | **Stocks in Play ORB**: Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz 2024, [SSRN 4729284 / SFI 24-98](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2498.html) ([Concretum page](https://concretumgroup.com/a-profitable-day-trading-strategy-for-the-u-s-equity-market/)) | A 5-minute ORB is traded only on names whose opening-range volume is ≥ 100% of the 14-day average, taking the top 20 by relative volume. The authors report it net of costs. These are the authors' own backtests. | A "quality reps" gate: send our opening setups to high-relative-volume names. | not run (needs opening-range volume from 1-min packs; proposed W6-SIP) |
| 10 | **Volatility-managed exposure**: Moreira & Muir, *JF* 72(4), 2017 ([NBER w22208](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208)) | Scaling exposure by 1/(last month's variance) raised factor Sharpe ratios, because expected returns do not rise in step with volatility. | Scale daily risk by SPY variance known before the open. This is a different mechanism from P8's record tilts. | X6 |

**Execution note (not counted in the ten).** Alpaca margin and short-selling docs ([search summary](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/margin-and-short-selling); [HTB launch post](https://alpaca.markets/blog/htb-trading-api-locates/)): ETB shorts have $0 borrow, and HTB needs an approved locate plus a daily fee. Per a [forum note](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/shortable-stock-vs-easy-to-borrow/3824), an open short is not closed when its name turns HTB. This supports the C3-MECH ETB universe. Nothing new was tested.

**Daily loss limits.** The search found no study showing that a daily loss limit improves day-trader results. Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean ([Haas PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)) document persistence, not the effect of stop rules. This agrees with P8, where daily caps cut R by 50-100%. No new test was run.
